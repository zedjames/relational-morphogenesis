# RMMO Publication Principles

## Every paper stands independently

Every RMMO manuscript must establish locally:

1. the biological object inherited by the paper;
2. the minimum prior result used as input;
3. the independent biological question;
4. the result newly established in the paper;
5. the remaining authority boundary.

A manuscript should remain scientifically intelligible and worthwhile if every later RMMO paper were removed.

## The series-level narrative is a hypothesis under attack

Narrative coherence is never used as evidence. Before drafting each paper, review actively tests whether the apparent result is created by representation, normalization, numerical approximation, null design, information leakage, or editorial selection.

The current series-level hypothesis is examined rather than assumed:

**relational law may persist while its realization changes.**

Each paper receives an independent opportunity to refute, narrow, or reformulate that hypothesis.

## Standalone publication gate

Before release:

- **Standalone question:** a systems biologist can understand why the experiment matters from this paper alone.
- **Standalone methods:** all nonstandard objects are defined locally.
- **Standalone evidence:** decisive results are present in this paper's public companion.
- **Standalone boundary:** a referee can determine exactly what the paper establishes from this paper alone.
- **Narrative independence:** the paper remains worth publishing if later papers do not exist.

## Negative and narrowing results

Frozen thresholds, failed gates, superseded interpretations, numerical corrections, and authority limitations are preserved when they materially affect a manuscript.
